MAAA Celebrates 50 Years!

Celebrating 50 years in 2026, the nonprofit Museum of African American Art (MAAA) in Los Angeles was founded by Dr. Samella Lewis in 1976 and educates visitors of all ages and identities about the arts through the lens of African American culture. Exhibitions and events at MAAA reflect the belief that museums are for everyone and continue the museum's founding principle that art can and should be part of our daily lives. MAAA offers FREE admission, serves as a community gathering space, and creates cultural experiences for students, families, and lifelong learners.

MAAA Digitization Project

MAKING the museum's HISTORY ACCESSIBLE

The Archives @ MAAA

As we continue to engage with the ongoing project of digitizing the archives and institutional records at the nonprofit Museum of African American Art, we are uncovering documents, photos, and ephemera that chronicle the museum's early years, give insight into relationships between MAAA founder Dr. Samella Lewis and other artists, and reveal connections among MAAA, arts organizations, and grantmaking institutions in Los Angeles. We look forward to digitizing and sharing primary source material from the MAAA archives, so that we can fully chronicle and authoritatively voice the museum's important history for all audiences, while supporting research by students, researchers, curators, authors, and documentary filmmakers.

from the archives

This hand-painted "MAAA" logo on wood panel is the museum's original logo design created circa 1979 by Howard Smith (1928-2021), an African American artist born in New Jersey who spent most of his career in Finland. Howard Smith was a friend of the Museum of African American Art's founder, Dr. Samella Lewis, and the museum's archives include transcripts of an interview between the two.

Museum Hours

Visit the Museum of African American Art's exhibit space inside Baldwin Hills Crenshaw Plaza on Level 2, Suite 283, across from TJ Maxx.
